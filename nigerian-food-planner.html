Nigerian family food budget planner

Finds the cheapest daily menu that still meets your family's nutrition needs, using linear programming.

Your family

Enter how many people are in each group.

Daily nutrition targets (per person)

Pick a group, then set its minimum and maximum for each nutrient. Clear a box to remove that limit.

Menu variety

Without limits the cheapest menu can be mostly one or two foods. These rules keep it balanced. Set a box to 0 to turn that rule off.

Foods your family won't eat

Tick any food to leave it out of the menu.

Language

Only food names change, and the local names are a best-effort translation. Please check them with a native speaker.

General price inflation

This raises every food by the same percentage. To give one food its own rate, type it in the "Change %" box next to that food in the price list. Leave a box empty to use the general rate.

Food prices (₦ per kg)

Rice, beans, garri, tomatoes, pepper and eggs start with recent official prices (Lagos State Ministry of Agriculture price tracker, October 2026, and the National Bureau of Statistics, April 2026). Every other food starts with an estimate. Prices differ by market and state, so replace them with what you pay. "Grams per cup" lets the app also show amounts in cups. The figures are rough estimates for one level cup of each food as bought, so weigh your own cup and edit them. Leave a box empty to hide cups for that food. Tins and congos are worked out from the same figure: spaghetti and macaroni also show as 500 g packs and eggs as pieces of about 50 g each (edit PACK in app.js if your packs differ). One milk tin is 140 ml (the Three Crowns tin; other brands differ), 10 tins make 1 congo, and a cup is 240 ml.

Nutrition values for most foods come from the FAO/INFOODS Food Composition Table for Western Africa (2019). Garri and instant noodles are approximate estimates, and spaghetti uses the table's dried macaroni values. Values are per 100 g, approximate, for raw or dry weights. The default targets are rough averages. This is a planning aid, not medical advice.

About this app and its sources

The app uses linear programming. It looks for the cheapest set of foods that meets every nutrition limit you set, using the simplex method, and recalculates whenever prices or limits change. Your settings are saved on this device only.

Sources: nutrient values from the FAO/INFOODS Food Composition Table for Western Africa (2019); prices from the Lagos State Ministry of Agriculture price tracker (October 2026) and the National Bureau of Statistics Selected Food Prices Watch (April 2026).

Estimates: most food prices, the nutrition values of garri and instant noodles, grams per cup, pack and egg sizes, and the portions shared between people.

Important: this is a planning aid, not medical advice. Pregnant or breastfeeding women, babies, older people and anyone with a health condition should follow the advice of a doctor, nurse or dietitian.
